# Source review: C3S Enhanced Windstorm Service v1.0

- Review date: **2026-08-10**
- Admission state: **metadata only**
- Manifest: `manifests/copernicus.c3s.european-windstorm-reanalysis.v1.0.json`
- Provider: Copernicus Climate Change Service (C3S), operated through ECMWF's Climate Data Store
- Product: `Windstorm tracks and footprints derived from reanalysis over Europe between 1940 to present`
- Product version: `1.0`
- DOI: `10.24381/bf1f06a9`

## Why this source is useful

This is a strong next source for the Germany/European-windstorm research path because it supplies an event-oriented hazard layer that the current DWD station admission does not provide. The product was developed as part of the C3S Enhanced Windstorm Service and explicitly responds to insurance-sector requirements.

The source exposes three distinct product families:

1. **windstorm tracks** — six-hourly storm trajectories generated by two tracking approaches, TempestExtremes and TRACK/Hodges;
2. **windstorm footprints** — maximum 10 m wind gust over a 72-hour event-centred window, on the ERA5 0.25° grid and a statistically downscaled approximately 0.016° grid, with full-domain and decontaminated-footprint configurations;
3. **annual summary indicators** — regional statistics including storm counts and severity indicators.

These products must not be collapsed into one generic "wind data" object. Track, footprint and annual-summary semantics are materially different.

## Source identity and mutability

The authoritative CDS dataset page identifies product version `1.0`, temporal coverage from 1940 to the present and a monthly update cycle. The STAC catalogue also exposes current temporal extent and licence metadata.

`1.0` identifies the product design/version; it does **not** freeze the bytes represented by the mutable phrase `to present`. A reproducible acquisition therefore needs more than the product version. It must record the exact CDS request or asset identity, including as applicable:

- product family;
- tracking algorithm;
- footprint resolution;
- full-domain versus decontaminated footprint configuration;
- event/time selection;
- any threshold or regional selection;
- returned filename or stable asset identifier;
- retrieval timestamp;
- byte size and SHA-256.

No such raw artifact is admitted by this review.

## Rights assessment

The authoritative CDS dataset/STAC metadata labels this dataset `CC-BY-4.0`. The CDS CC-BY licence page points to the Creative Commons Attribution licence, and the CDS terms state that content is used under the licence attributed to the product. Download access requires CDS registration.

Engineering interpretation for the reviewed product:

- licence identity: `CC-BY-4.0`;
- commercial use: permitted by CC BY 4.0;
- redistribution/adaptation: permitted subject to attribution and licence conditions;
- access: registration is required for download through the CDS;
- endorsement: use must not imply ECMWF/EU endorsement.

OpenCatastrophe deliberately approves **metadata only** at this stage even though source rights are broader. This prevents a monthly-changing catalogue or an unspecified CDS query from being mistaken for a reproducible admitted artifact.

## Scientific semantics

### Event identity

The source provides storm tracks selected by tracking algorithms. A future adapter must keep at least the source event identifier, algorithm identity and product version visible. Events from different tracking algorithms must not be merged merely because names, dates or tracks appear similar.

### Hazard footprint

A footprint is a maximum 10 m wind-gust field over a 72-hour time window centred on the tracked storm's maximum 925 hPa wind-speed time step. This is not interchangeable with:

- a point station gust observation;
- an instantaneous model field;
- a return-period map;
- a vulnerability function;
- an insured-loss footprint.

The physical intensity unit is metres per second. Grid resolution and footprint configuration are part of the artifact semantics and must remain explicit.

### Frequency and annual metrics

The event catalogue and the annual summary product have different roles. A tracked historical/reanalysis event is not assigned a new annual rate by OpenCatastrophe merely because it appears in the catalogue. Any future stochastic/frequency model must be separately specified and validated.

Annual storm-count and severity indicators are useful validation or descriptive evidence, but they are not substitutes for an event occurrence model or a year-loss table.

## Relationship to DWD observations

The current DWD admission is a useful independent observational route for Germany station-level wind validation. A future comparison may evaluate C3S event timing and local gust magnitudes against DWD observations, but the two sources have different spatial support and measurement/model semantics:

- DWD: station observations with station/instrument history and quality-state distinctions;
- C3S EWS: ERA5-derived event tracks and gridded footprints, including statistical downscaling.

Agreement or disagreement must therefore be interpreted through explicit spatial/temporal matching rather than treating either source as byte-level truth for the other.

## Suitable initial OpenCatastrophe uses

Good initial uses:

- European/Germany windstorm event discovery;
- hazard-footprint research at explicitly selected resolution;
- comparison of two tracking algorithms;
- event-level validation against independent station observations;
- testing event/footprint provenance and adapter contracts;
- later exposure-overlay experiments once an exposure source is separately admitted.

Not sufficient by itself for:

- insured exposure or total insured value;
- vulnerability or damage functions;
- claims calibration;
- policy/reinsurance terms;
- a production-grade stochastic event set;
- pricing, capital or regulatory claims.

## Requirements before raw admission

Before any C3S EWS bytes can move beyond metadata-only status, a proposal must:

1. re-check the current dataset licence and CDS terms;
2. pin the exact request/asset and all scientifically material selectors;
3. acquire the bytes outside Git;
4. record byte size and SHA-256;
5. inspect source metadata for exact grid, time, units and conventions;
6. define whether the artifact is track, footprint or annual-summary data;
7. record any transformation independently from acquisition;
8. obtain explicit asset-specific publication review.

Until then, no C3S raw or derived bytes belong in this repository.
